# Audit — every screen and every route

The inventory of what the application exposes, what each thing requires, and
what it returns. It is the document that answers "can this member reach
this, and what do they get when they do?"

**Status date:** 2026-11-16
**How it was produced:** read from the source, then checked against the
running application. The route list is generated by
`apps/backend/src/common/constants/route-inventory.spec.ts`, so it cannot
drift from the code without a test failing.

---

## 1. The three layers of authorisation

A member's reach is decided in three places, and they are not
interchangeable.

| Layer | Question it answers | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Permission** | *What kind of action is this?* | `@Permissions(...)` / `@AnyPermission(...)` on the route |
| **Scope** | *Which records may this action touch?* | `AuthorisationService`, `ScopeService`, and the module's own scope service |
| **Field** | *Which parts of the record may be seen?* | The service assembles the response; a part the member may not see is **absent**, not blank |

A record the member may not see must not appear **anywhere** — not in the
list, not in the response, not in a column the frontend hides.

**The rule about "any" and "all".** A route that names one permission
requires that permission. A route that names several, under
`@Permissions`, requires **all** of them — the usual case, because naming
several usually means "and". A route that several roles use through
*different* permissions says so with `@AnyPermission(...)`, which requires
**at least one**.

`route-reachability.spec.ts` asserts that every role holding at least one
of a route's permissions can open that route, so a route cannot lock out
the very people it was written for.

### What the matrix says about each role

**Super Admin — configuration, not teaching.** Reads the academic data of
the whole college; configures members, roles, branches, schemes, academic
years and subject offerings. Holds **no** `students:write`, `marks:write` or
`upload:create` — the exam cell's work is not the administrator's work.
Holds no allocation permission either: who teaches what is the HOD's
decision.

**Admin (the exam cell) — the people who enter.** Creates students and
subjects, enters marks, approves a whole extraction, generates and
downloads reports, publishes notifications. Reads the sensitive identity
data and the guardian details, because it is the role that records them in
the first place.

**HOD — the branch.** Reads the students and the marks of their branch,
generates reports, writes subjects and subject offerings, and allocates
teachers and class teachers. Branch-scoped: the branch comes from their
role assignment, never from a parameter.

**Principal — read-only.** Thirteen permissions, no write of any kind.
Reads the academic picture, including the audit trail, but not the sensitive
identity data.

**Teacher — the allocated subjects.** Enters marks only for the subjects
the HOD allocated to them, sees only their own students and their own
class's statistics, and allocates nothing.

**Student — their own record.** Four permissions: the dashboard, their own
result, their own marks, their own class's toppers. The college's roster,
the mark sheet, the reports and the administration are all closed to them.

**Who writes a mark.** The exam cell defines what a subject is worth; the
teacher writes what a student scored.

- A **teacher** may write only for the offerings the HOD allocated to them,
  for that academic year. Class-teacher status grants nothing here.
- The **exam cell** may write any offering in scope — but **not** one a
  teacher holds an allocation for. That is a *correction*: a separate
  operation that needs a reason, is recorded under its own audit action,
  keeps the value it replaced on a revision row, and names the teacher whose
  work it replaced.

## 5. The gaps this audit found, and what was done

| # | Gap | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `GET /marks` was closed to the HOD and the Principal: the route named two permissions under an "all of" rule, and neither role holds both. | **Fixed.** `@AnyPermission`; ten further instances found and fixed at the same time. |
| 2 | `POST /subject-offerings` guarded on `subjects:write` while the catalogue gives an offering its own permission. | **Fixed.** The offering routes use the offering permissions. |
| 3 | The widened `Student` model disagreed with its own migration — four columns had no `@map`, so every read of a student failed with a 500. | **Fixed.** The schema and the migration are one thing again. |
| 4 | The correction flow existed on the server but had no screen; the exam cell had no route to a correction. | **Fixed.** `/marks/correct`, with a reason, a revision row, and the teacher named. |
| 5 | The consequence preview existed on the server but the UI called `deactivate` directly, and a raw `window.confirm` said a soft delete "permanently deletes". | **Fixed.** The revocation dialog shows the consequences before anything changes. |
| 6 | "Class teacher required" was a phrase in a comment and nowhere else; a class with no teacher did not appear anywhere. | **Fixed.** `GET /class-teacher-assignments/needs-teacher`, and a panel on the assignments screen. |
| 7 | Reports were **hard-deleted**. | **Fixed.** They are archived; the row is kept with the reason and who archived it. |
| 8 | The scheme form asked an administrator to type raw JSON. | **Fixed.** Structured, labelled inputs; the numbers are derived and confirmed. |
| 9 | `POST /upload/jobs/:jobId/confirm` — the route that **writes marks** — was guarded on `upload:read`, a permission to *look* at an upload. | **Fixed.** Guarded on the two write permissions, and the exam cell can no longer overwrite a teacher's subject through an upload. |
| 10 | An academic year could only be added by re-running the seed. | **Fixed.** `POST /academic/years`, with the running year derived from the dates. |
| 11 | The `interceptors.ts` file was a placeholder (`export {}`), and there was no 422 or network case in the error mapping. | **Fixed.** A real failure classifier, with tests. |
| 12 | **A student's own result never loaded.** The frontend called `/students/my-result`; the server serves `/my-result`. | **Fixed.** The request fell through to the roster's `GET /students/:id` and was refused. |
| 13 | **A whole class's marks timed out.** The bulk write of twenty students across six subjects exceeded the thirty-second default. | **Fixed.** The bulk route declares a two-minute timeout on both sides, and the write is still **whole**. |
| 14 | **The examination lifecycle was modelled but not exposed.** `Examination` had a lifecycle and two permissions; no module, route or screen used them. | **Fixed.** The `examinations` module, five routes and the "Publish results" screen are in place, and the whole cycle is verified end to end. |
| 15 | **The academic-years screen had no create button**, though the route existed. | **Fixed.** The screen opens a year with its dates and can change which year is running. |
| 16 | **The students list removed a student behind a browser prompt** that said nothing about the consequence. | **Fixed.** A dialog that says the student is archived and their marks are kept. |
